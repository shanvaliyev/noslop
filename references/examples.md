# Worked examples

Full rewrites across formats, each with a short note on what changed. Use these to calibrate how hard to edit. Some examples call for heavy rewrites, some for two small changes, and one shows a "fix" that made things worse.

Every specific detail in an "After" comes from the brief, the source notes, or elsewhere in the draft. When a rewrite needed something that wasn't available, the note says so.

## 1. Write mode: LinkedIn launch post from a brief

**Brief:** "LinkedIn post announcing Ledgerly's new invoice reminders. Took 4 months. Beta with 60 freelancers. Average time to get paid dropped from 34 to 19 days. Reminders send from the user's own email address. Free on all plans. I'm the founder, Priya."

**Default model output:**

> 🚀 Exciting news! 🚀
>
> I'm thrilled to announce that Ledgerly just launched Smart Reminders!
>
> Getting paid shouldn't be hard. But for freelancers, it often is.
>
> That's why we built something different. Not just another reminder tool. A game-changer for your cash flow.
>
> ✅ Automated follow-ups
> ✅ Personalized messaging
> ✅ Seamless integration
>
> The result? Our beta users got paid 44% faster. 💸
>
> This is just the beginning.
>
> Agree? Drop a 🙌 below!
>
> #Freelancing #Fintech #Startups

**noslop output:**

> Our beta users got paid in 19 days instead of 34.
>
> The only change: Ledgerly started chasing their late invoices for them. 🎉
>
> When a client misses a due date, Ledgerly sends a reminder from your own email address, in the same thread as the invoice. To the client, it looks like you followed up yourself.
>
> We spent four months on it and tested it with 60 freelancers.
>
> It's live today, free on every plan.
>
> Freelancers: how long does your slowest client usually take to pay?

**What changed:** It keeps a hook, short lines, one emoji for a real launch, and a question at the end, because those work on LinkedIn. The changes are in the content. The hook is the brief's best number instead of "Exciting news!" The three checkmark bullets became the one mechanism that matters: the reminder comes from your own address. Replaced the computed "44% faster" with the real 34-to-19-day figures. Cut the not-X-but-Y line, "game-changer," the "just the beginning" kicker, the emoji wall and the hashtags. "Agree? Drop a 🙌" became a question freelancers can actually answer. The colon in line two is a pacing reveal, which is fine on social and would be flattened on a website.

**Not added:** a beta user quote. The brief didn't have one, so the reply to Priya asks for one.

## 2. Blog intro

**Before:**

> In today's fast-paced digital landscape, remote work has become more than just a trend — it's a fundamental shift in how we think about productivity. But here's the thing: most teams are doing it wrong. In this post, we'll delve into five key strategies that can help your team thrive in a remote-first world. Let's dive in!

(Further down, the draft says the writer's 12-person team cancelled every recurring meeting for a month and brought back only three.)

**After:**

> Last spring my team of 12 cancelled every recurring meeting for a month. When the month ended, we brought back three. That experiment is the first of five changes below, and it's the one I'd start with, because it costs nothing and you'll know within a week whether it's working.

**What changed:** The original intro could open any remote-work post on the internet, so it fails the portability test. The rewrite moves the draft's best specific detail to the top. Cut "in today's fast-paced digital landscape," the not-X-but-Y contrast, "here's the thing," the unsupported "most teams are doing it wrong," "delve," the signposting and "let's dive in." It also removes the em dash.

## 3. Landing page hero and features

**Source notes:** Scheduling tool for dental clinics. Patients book from a text message link. No-show rate at pilot clinics dropped from 18% to 7%. Syncs with Dentrix and Open Dental. Setup is done by the company, takes one afternoon. $149/month per location.

**Before:**

> # Revolutionize Your Practice with Seamless Scheduling
>
> Unlock the power of intelligent scheduling and elevate the patient experience. Our cutting-edge platform empowers dental practices to streamline operations, reduce no-shows, and drive growth.
>
> **✨ Effortless Booking** — Patients book in seconds, anytime, anywhere.
> **🔗 Seamless Integration** — Works with the tools you already love.
> **📈 Proven Results** — Practices see dramatic improvements in attendance.
>
> Whether you're a solo practitioner or a multi-location group, we've got you covered.

**After:**

> # Patients book from a text. Fewer of them forget to show up.
>
> We send each patient a link by text. They pick a time from your real availability, and it lands straight in Dentrix or Open Dental. Reminders go out the same way.
>
> At our pilot clinics, no-shows fell from 18% to 7%.
>
> We do the setup ourselves. It takes one afternoon, and your front desk doesn't have to learn a new system.
>
> $149 a month per location.

**What changed:** Every claim in the "before" fits any scheduling product. The rewrite uses the facts from the notes: the text link, the two named integrations, the no-show numbers, the setup and the price. The bold-label emoji bullets became short paragraphs because each feature needed a sentence of explanation. Cut the reader sweep ("whether you're a solo practitioner...") and the em dashes. The headline now says what happens to patients.

## 4. Cold outreach email

**Source notes:** Sender: Tom, runs a two-person studio that makes product demo videos. Recipient: head of marketing at a dev-tools startup whose homepage has no video. Tom's last client saw trial signups from the homepage go up after adding a 90-second demo (Tom has the number: 23%). Ask: a 15-minute call.

**Before:**

> Subject: Elevate Your Brand Story 🎬
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> I hope this email finds you well! I came across your company and was truly impressed by the innovative work you're doing in the developer tools space.
>
> I wanted to reach out because I believe compelling video content can be a game-changer for companies like yours. In today's crowded market, standing out is more important than ever.
>
> At Brightframe Studio, we specialize in crafting engaging, high-quality demo videos that resonate with technical audiences and drive meaningful results.
>
> Would you be open to a quick call to explore potential synergies? Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions!
>
> Best regards,
> Tom

**After:**

> Subject: 90-second demo for your homepage?
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> Your homepage explains the CLI in text and a code sample, but there's no video of it running. Last quarter we made a 90-second demo for another dev-tools company, and trial signups from their homepage went up 23% in the month after it went live.
>
> I'd like to show you that video and talk through what one for your product could look like. Would 15 minutes next week work?
>
> Tom
> Brightframe Studio

**What changed:** Opened with something Tom actually noticed about Sarah's site, which proves the email isn't a mass send. Replaced "drive meaningful results" with the real 23% figure. Kept one clear ask. Cut "I hope this email finds you well," "truly impressed," "game-changer," "in today's crowded market," "synergies" and "don't hesitate." The subject line names the specific offer.

## 5. Over-correction: when the fix is also slop

**Original:**

> It's important to note that while AI tools can significantly enhance productivity, they're not a silver bullet. Teams that thrive are those that thoughtfully integrate these tools into existing workflows, fostering a culture of experimentation while maintaining human oversight.

**Over-corrected (strip everything, punch everything):**

> AI tools help. They're not magic. Good teams experiment. Humans stay in charge. That's it.

**Why this fails:** It deleted the slop words and then rebuilt the slop rhythm: stacked fragments, a negation, and a "That's it" kicker. It still says nothing specific, and it reads like a different machine.

**Better:**

> AI tools speed up the parts of our work that were already well defined, like first drafts of support replies and test scaffolding. They haven't helped much with work where we're still figuring out what we want. We let anyone try a new tool for two weeks, and a person still reviews anything that goes to a customer.

**What changed:** Replaced abstractions with specific claims about where the tools help and where they don't, plus the actual policy. The sentences run longer because the thoughts have parts. If the writer hadn't supplied "support replies," "test scaffolding" or the two-week rule, the right move would be to ask the writer for examples.

## 6. Light touch: a rough draft that's mostly fine

**Before (founder's newsletter, their own voice):**

> ok so I'll be honest, the redesign was a mess. we shipped it on a friday (never again) and by saturday morning half our power users were in the discord asking where the export button went. it's in the overflow menu now, which made sense in figma and makes zero sense in real life. we're moving it back this week. lesson learned, test with the people who actually click the button 40 times a day. It's important to note that we value your feedback and are committed to continuous improvement.

**After:**

> ok so I'll be honest, the redesign was a mess. we shipped it on a friday (never again) and by saturday morning half our power users were in the discord asking where the export button went. it's in the overflow menu now, which made sense in figma and makes zero sense in real life. we're moving it back this week. lesson learned: test with the people who actually click the button 40 times a day.

**What changed:** Deleted the last sentence, which is corporate boilerplate the writer didn't write in their own voice. Changed one comma to a colon for clarity. The lowercase, the "I'll be honest," the parenthetical and the casual "lesson learned" all stay. They're the writer's voice and they carry specific information. "I'll be honest" here leads into a real admission, so it isn't a staged opener.

## 7. Newsletter item with puffery

**Before:**

> Local favorite Rosa's Kitchen celebrated its 25th anniversary this month, marking a significant milestone in the vibrant culinary landscape of East Austin. Nestled on Cesar Chavez Street, the beloved eatery has long stood as a testament to the enduring power of family recipes, showcasing authentic flavors that have captivated generations of diners. Owner Rosa Delgado's unwavering commitment to quality underscores the restaurant's lasting legacy.

**After:**

> Rosa's Kitchen on Cesar Chavez Street turned 25 this month. Rosa Delgado still makes the tamales from her mother's recipe, and on weekends the line for them starts before the doors open at 8.

**What changed:** Cut "significant milestone," "vibrant culinary landscape," "nestled," "stood as a testament," "showcasing," "captivated generations," "unwavering commitment," "underscores" and "lasting legacy." The tamale recipe and the weekend line came from the reporter's notes. Without notes, the honest version is one sentence: "Rosa's Kitchen on Cesar Chavez Street turned 25 this month."

## 8. A reply that re-explains everything

**Context:** In a Slack thread, a designer asked, "Can we push the launch to Thursday? The empty states aren't done."

**Before:**

> Thanks for raising this! I understand that the empty states are an important part of the user experience, and I agree that launching without them could create a confusing first impression for new users. I've looked at the timeline, and pushing to Thursday would mean we miss the newsletter send on Wednesday, which is our biggest acquisition channel. However, I think quality is important. Let me check with marketing and get back to you. Does that work?

**After:**

> Thursday means missing Wednesday's newsletter, which is where most of our signups come from. Could we launch Wednesday with plain-text empty states and swap in the real ones Thursday? If that's not workable I'll ask marketing about moving the send.

**What changed:** The designer already knows why empty states matter, so the restatement goes. The reply leads with the one fact the designer may not have (the newsletter conflict), offers a concrete option, and keeps the fallback. Cut "Thanks for raising this," "I understand that," "I think quality is important" and "Does that work?"

## 9. Essay ending

**Before:**

> In conclusion, building a startup is a marathon, not a sprint. The founders who succeed aren't necessarily the smartest or the best-funded. They're the ones who keep showing up, day after day, even when it's hard. Because at the end of the day, persistence isn't just a strategy. It's everything.

(The essay's last body paragraph describes the writer nearly shutting the company down in month 14, then landing their first enterprise customer in month 16.)

**After:**

> I came close to shutting the company down in month 14. I'd drafted the email to our three employees. Our first enterprise contract came in month 16, from a lead I'd almost stopped emailing. I still don't know if that was persistence or luck.

**What changed:** The original ending is a pile of stock lines: "In conclusion," a marathon metaphor, not-X-but-Y twice, "day after day," "at the end of the day" and a kicker. The rewrite ends on the essay's own best story and admits uncertainty plainly. Note that the drafted email to employees comes from the essay body. If it hadn't, the ending would stop at "month 16."

## 10. Company update with bold bullets

**Before:**

> ## Q3 Highlights 🎉
>
> This quarter was truly transformative for our team. Here are the key highlights:
>
> - **🚀 Growth:** We achieved remarkable growth, with revenue increasing significantly.
> - **🤝 Partnerships:** We forged strategic partnerships that will shape our future.
> - **💡 Innovation:** We launched groundbreaking features that delighted our users.
> - **👥 Team:** We welcomed amazing new talent to our growing family.
>
> We're incredibly proud of what we've accomplished together, and the future has never looked brighter!

**Source notes:** Revenue $410k to $520k. Partnership with Gusto (payroll integration, live in October). Launched bulk editing (most requested feature, 340 votes). Hired two engineers and a support lead.

**After:**

> ## Q3
>
> Revenue went from $410k to $520k.
>
> We shipped bulk editing, the most requested feature on the board with 340 votes. The Gusto payroll integration is signed and goes live in October.
>
> Two engineers and a support lead joined, so support replies should get faster this quarter.

**What changed:** Each bullet was a label and a vague claim, so the rewrite turns them into the facts from the notes. Cut "truly transformative," "remarkable," "significantly," "forged strategic partnerships," "groundbreaking," "delighted," "amazing new talent," "growing family" and the "future has never looked brighter" send-off. The four parallel bullets became three short paragraphs grouped by what goes together. The line about support replies is the one consequence a reader would care about. It's an inference, so check it with the writer before publishing.

## 11. Matching a voice sample

**Writer's sample (from their past posts):**

> I've been a carpenter for nineteen years — long enough to have opinions about screws. Most of them are bad opinions. A few of them are right, and those I'll fight you on.

**Draft to fix (model-written, for the same writer):**

> As an experienced carpenter, I've learned that choosing the right fasteners is crucial for any project. Deck screws offer superior holding power, while nails provide flexibility. Ultimately, the best choice depends on your specific needs and project requirements.

**After:**

> Use deck screws for a deck. Nails go in faster and flex with the wood, but the wood moves every season and works them loose, so you're out there with a hammer every spring. Screws cost more and take longer, and you only drive them once.

**What changed:** Matched the sample: short declaratives next to longer ones, dry humor, and a willingness to take a side. Replaced the false balance ("depends on your specific needs") with the opinion this writer clearly holds. The sample uses em dashes, so one would be fine here, but this version didn't need one. Cut "As an experienced carpenter" (the sample already shows the experience), "crucial," "superior," "Ultimately" and "specific needs and project requirements." It was tempting to add an anecdote, like a neighbor's deck, and that would have been invented. Ask the writer for one instead.

## 12. Audit mode output

**Text submitted:** "Is this AI? Don't rewrite it, just tell me."

> Our new wellness program isn't just about fitness — it's about transforming lives. Designed with meticulous care, it seamlessly integrates mindfulness, nutrition, and movement into one holistic experience. Whether you're a busy professional or a stay-at-home parent, you'll discover a path to your best self. The best part? It's completely free. Ready to start your journey?

**Audit:**

> 1. Not X but Y: "isn't just about fitness — it's about transforming lives". Fix: say what the program does.
> 2. Em dash: in the same sentence. Fix: comma or rewrite.
> 3. Stock words: "meticulous," "seamlessly," "holistic," "journey". Fix: plain words or concrete details.
> 4. Forced triad: "mindfulness, nutrition, and movement". Fix: keep only if all three are real parts; say what each involves.
> 5. Reader sweep: "Whether you're a busy professional or a stay-at-home parent". Fix: write to one reader.
> 6. Inflation: "a path to your best self". Fix: state the actual outcome.
> 7. Self-answered question: "The best part? It's completely free." Fix: "It's free."
> 8. Engagement closer: "Ready to start your journey?" Fix: end on how to sign up.
>
> Main habits: staged contrast and inflated promises, with no specifics anywhere. Eight patterns in five sentences is a lot. Want me to edit it? I'd need what the program actually includes, how long it runs and who it's for.

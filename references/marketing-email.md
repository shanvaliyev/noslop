# Marketing copy and email

Readers come to marketing copy already skeptical. A specific claim (a number, a price, a mechanism, a named customer) gives them a reason to keep reading. With email, the reader also decides from the first line whether this message deserves their time.

## Landing pages and product copy

### How it works

- **The headline says what the product does, or what changes for the user.** "Invoices that chase themselves" or "Send payroll in 10 minutes" beats "The Future of Finance."
- **Prove one claim.** "Setup takes 4 minutes" does more than "seamless, intuitive, and effortless."
- **Explain the mechanism.** How does it actually work? Readers trust copy that shows the gears.
- **Name the reader.** Write for one kind of buyer. "For clinics with two to ten chairs" beats "Whether you're a solo practitioner or a large enterprise."
- **Show the price, or say plainly why you don't.**
- **Give each feature a sentence.** "Bulk edit: change the price on 500 products at once" beats "⚡ Bulk Actions."
- **Name the customer and the result.** A named customer with a result beats "Trusted by thousands of teams."

### Tells specific to marketing

| Tell | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Invitation openers | "Discover the power of..." "Unlock your potential" "Elevate your..." "Say hello to..." "Meet X, your new..." | Say what it does |
| Imagine openers | "Imagine a world where..." "What if you could..." | State the world. "Your invoices send themselves." |
| Stacked benefits | "Fast, secure, and scalable." "Simple. Powerful. Affordable." | One measurable claim per benefit, or pick the one that matters |
| Vague nouns | solutions, platform, ecosystem, experience, journey, capabilities | Name the product and the action |
| Empty superlatives | best-in-class, world-class, industry-leading, revolutionary, next-generation | The comparison with evidence, or cut |
| Fake urgency | "Don't miss out!" "Limited time!" (when it isn't) | Give the real deadline, or remove it |
| Reader sweep | "Whether you're a startup or an enterprise..." "From freelancers to Fortune 500s" | Pick the reader |
| "Reimagined" | "Email, reimagined." "Banking, redefined." | Say what's different |
| Emoji feature grids | 🚀 Speed / 🔒 Security / 💡 Smart | A sentence per feature saying what it does |
| Unearned "we" excitement | "We're thrilled to introduce..." | Lead with what the user gets |

### Example: feature section

**Before:**

> ## Powerful Features to Supercharge Your Workflow
>
> **⚡ Lightning-Fast Performance** — Experience blazing speed that keeps up with your ambitions.
>
> **🔒 Enterprise-Grade Security** — Rest easy knowing your data is protected by industry-leading security.
>
> **🤝 Seamless Collaboration** — Work together effortlessly, no matter where your team is.

**Source notes:** Search returns in under 100ms on 1M documents. SOC 2 Type II certified, data encrypted at rest with AES-256. Real-time co-editing, comments, and a per-document permission system.

**After:**

> ## What you get
>
> **Search that keeps up.** Results in under 100 ms, even across a million documents.
>
> **SOC 2 Type II.** Data is encrypted at rest with AES-256. The report is available on request.
>
> **Editing together.** Several people can edit one document at the same time, leave comments, and set who can see each document.

Bold labels are fine on a landing page when each label is short and the sentence after it adds facts. The tell is a label that only restates what the sentence already says.

## Email

### How it works

- **The ask, or the news, goes in the first two lines.** Many readers only see the preview.
- **The subject line says what's inside.** "Contract for Thursday's shoot" beats "Quick question" or "Touching base 👋."
- **Make one ask per email.** If there are two, number them.
- **Write it the way you'd say it across a desk.** Contractions, short paragraphs, plain words.
- **Sign off with your name.** "Thanks" is fine. "Please don't hesitate to reach out with any questions" isn't.
- **Follow-ups add something,** such as a new detail, a deadline or a simpler ask.

### Tells specific to email

| Tell | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wellness opener | "I hope this email finds you well." "I hope you're having a great week!" | Delete. Start with the reason you're writing |
| Reaching out | "I wanted to reach out..." "I'm reaching out because..." | State the reason directly |
| Fake flattery | "I came across your profile and was truly impressed by..." | Mention one specific thing you actually noticed, or nothing |
| Circling back | "Just circling back." "Bumping this up." "Following up on my previous email." | Add new information or a smaller ask |
| Synergy close | "Would love to explore potential synergies." "Let's find time to connect." | A specific ask: "Would 15 minutes Tuesday work?" |
| Don't hesitate | "Please don't hesitate to reach out." "Feel free to let me know." | Cut. They know how email works |
| Excited newsletter voice | "We're thrilled to share some exciting updates with our amazing community!" | Lead with the most useful update |
| Uniform paragraphs | Every paragraph exactly three sentences | Vary. A one-line paragraph is fine |

### Example: customer announcement email

**Before:**

> Subject: Exciting Updates to Your Experience! 🎉
>
> Hi there,
>
> We hope this email finds you well! We're thrilled to share some exciting changes we've made to enhance your experience with our platform.
>
> As part of our ongoing commitment to providing exceptional value, we're updating our pricing structure, effective March 1st. This change will enable us to continue delivering innovative features and world-class support.
>
> We truly value your partnership and are excited about the journey ahead. Please don't hesitate to reach out with any questions!
>
> Warm regards,
> The Team

**Source notes:** Price going from $12 to $15/user/month on March 1. Existing annual plans keep their current price until renewal. Reason: last fall they moved all EU customer data to their own servers in Frankfurt for data residency, and those cost more than expected. Customers can lock in $12 by switching to annual before March 1.

**After:**

> Subject: Price change on March 1, and how to keep the current price
>
> Hi,
>
> On March 1 our price goes from $12 to $15 per user per month.
>
> We're raising it because we moved all EU customer data to our own servers in Frankfurt last fall so it never leaves the EU, and running them costs more than we expected.
>
> If you're on an annual plan, nothing changes until your renewal date. If you're on a monthly plan and switch to annual before March 1, you keep $12 for the next year. You can switch under Settings → Billing.
>
> Reply to this email with any questions. It comes straight to me.
>
> Dana, CEO

The arrow in "Settings → Billing" is a UI path, not decoration, so it stays.

### Example: follow-up email

**Before:**

> Subject: Re: Partnership opportunity
>
> Hi Marcus,
>
> Just circling back on my previous email! I know you're busy, but I wanted to bump this to the top of your inbox. Would love to find some time to connect and explore how we might work together.
>
> Looking forward to hearing from you!

**After:**

> Subject: Re: Partnership opportunity
>
> Hi Marcus,
>
> One more thing since I last wrote: we just shipped a Shopify app, so your merchants could add our returns flow without a developer. If that changes the picture, I can send a two-minute video instead of a call.
>
> Jen

This version only works if the Shopify app is real. A follow-up with nothing new to say should be one line ("Still interested in talking? If not, no worries.") or not sent at all.

# Social posts: X, LinkedIn, Threads, Bluesky

Social feeds play by different rules from the rest of this skill. The reader is scrolling, so the first line has about a second to earn attention. Hooks, line breaks, punchy closers and questions to the reader exist because they work there. Don't strip them out and hand back a polished paragraph. That reads like a press release and gets scrolled past.

A post reads as slop when the mechanics wrap nothing, or when the wording is a line the reader has already seen 500 times. Keep the craft and put something real inside it.

## Platform craft to keep

**Hooks.** Most readers decide from the first line. These hook types work, and each one is stronger with a specific in it:

| Hook type | Generic (reads as AI) | Specific (works) |
|---|---|---|
| Bold claim | "Most startup advice is wrong." | "'Talk to 100 customers first' cost me three months." |
| Number | "I learned so much this year." | "We threw out 30 loaves a day. Last week: almost zero." |
| Contrast | "AI won't replace you. People using AI will." | "Claude wrote 2,000 lines of our migration in an afternoon. I spent the next two days reading them." |
| Confession | "I failed. Here's what I learned." | "I priced our product at $19 for two years. A customer told me she'd have paid $60." |
| Scene | "Let me tell you a story." | "At 2 a.m. on launch night our payment page started returning 404s." |

The contrast hook is fine on social, but the not-X-but-Y rule still bites when both halves are vague.

**Line breaks and short lines.** These are normal on LinkedIn and X. Use them for pace: a short line for the turn, a longer one for the detail. The tell is every line the same length with no change in pace, or a dramatic one-word line with nothing behind it.

**Punchy closers.** A last line that lands is fine. It should add something: a consequence, a callback to the hook, a twist on the number. A closer that restates the post in aphorism form ("Consistency is everything.") is the slop version.

**Questions to readers.** These work when people can actually answer them. "What did you charge your first client?" gets replies. "Thoughts?" and "Agree?" are the lazy versions. They still work a little, but they signal autopilot.

**Hype.** Excitement is fine when the news is real. Match the energy to the news: a launch earns it, a minor update doesn't.

**Threads.** Numbering (1/, 2/) is fine. Each post in the thread should hold one real point. Make the first post the hook with the best specific, not "A thread 🧵👇" on its own.

**CTAs.** "Comment X and I'll send it" and "Repost if useful" are platform tactics. Use them if the user wants them, at most once per post.

## Still slop, even on social

| Tell | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Empty hook | A bold claim the post never backs up | Back it with the specific, or soften the claim |
| Recycled lines | "Let that sink in." "Read that again." "Here's what nobody tells you." "This changed everything." "X is dead." | A fresh version of the same move. Readers recognize the stock phrase instantly |
| Invented story or numbers | Made-up anecdotes, "my 6-year-old taught me about leadership" posts that didn't happen | Use what really happened, or ask the user |
| Platitude listicle | "5 lessons from 5 years:" each one generic | One lesson, with the story that taught it |
| Stock AI words | delve, game-changer, unlock, transformative, journey | Plain words |
| Emoji walls | ✅ 🚀 💡 starting every line, emoji after every sentence | One or two where the writer would use them, or none |
| Fake-bold Unicode | 𝗧𝗵𝗶𝘀 𝗶𝘀 𝗶𝗺𝗽𝗼𝗿𝘁𝗮𝗻𝘁 | Plain text. Screen readers turn it into gibberish |
| Hashtag walls | #Leadership #Growth #Mindset #Success | Zero to three, and only ones people actually follow |
| Mandatory redemption arc | Every failure ends in gratitude and a lesson | Let some things stay bad. Say what you'd still do differently |
| Label for honesty | "I'll be honest." "Can I be vulnerable for a sec?" before something routine | Fine before a real admission. Cut it before a routine claim |
| Chatbot residue | "Here's a post you can use:" left in the text | Delete |

## Platform notes

- **X:** The first line is the whole pitch. Cut for length before adding completeness. One idea per post. Threads should earn each additional post.
- **LinkedIn:** About two lines show before "...see more," so the hook has to finish inside them. Line breaks are expected. A personal story with a real number is the native format.
- **Threads and Bluesky:** More conversational and less performative. Heavy LinkedIn-style hooks can read as out of place there.

## Examples

### X post: keep the structure, add substance

**Original:**

> AI won't replace developers.
>
> But developers who use AI will replace developers who don't.
>
> Read that again. 🔥

This structure works on X, but the content is recycled. "AI won't replace you, people using AI will" has circulated in some form since 2023, and "Read that again" is one of the most recognizable closers on X. It can still get reach, though nobody will remember who wrote it.

**Same structure, real substance (if the writer has the example):**

> AI didn't replace me this week.
>
> It wrote 2,000 lines of our Postgres migration in an afternoon.
>
> I spent the next two days reading every one of them.

The hook and the line breaks stay. The post ends on the specific detail, which lands harder than a slogan closer would.

If the writer has no example and wants the original line, that's their call. Flag that it's a widely recycled line and offer to make it theirs.

### LinkedIn opinion post

**Before:**

> Unpopular opinion: Most startup advice is wrong.
>
> Not because the advice is bad.
>
> Because context matters.
>
> What worked for Airbnb won't work for you.
>
> Stop copying. Start thinking.
>
> Agree? 👇

**Source notes:** Writer followed "talk to 100 customers before building." It cost three months. Their buyers were hospital procurement teams who wouldn't take calls without a product to see. Within two weeks of having a rough prototype, they'd learned more than in the three months before.

**After:**

> "Talk to 100 customers before you build anything" cost me three months.
>
> We sell to hospital procurement teams.
>
> Almost none of them would take a call about a product that didn't exist. The few who did asked when they could see a demo.
>
> Two weeks after we had a rough prototype, we'd learned more than in the three months before it.
>
> The advice is good for consumer apps. It assumes your buyers have time to talk about hypotheticals.
>
> Mine didn't.
>
> Who else had a piece of standard advice backfire because of who you sell to?

The format is still LinkedIn native: short lines, a hook built for "see more," a closer that lands, and a question at the end. The only change is that every line carries something.

### Career update

**Before:**

> I'm thrilled to share that I'm starting a new position as Senior Product Designer at Figma! 🎉
>
> This journey wouldn't have been possible without the incredible mentors and colleagues who supported me along the way.
>
> Onwards and upwards! 🚀
>
> #NewJob #ProductDesign #Grateful

**Source notes:** Joining Figma's prototyping team. Previously four years designing a fintech mobile app. Got there by filing a long bug report about prototype transitions that someone on the team replied to.

**After:**

> Last year I filed an overly long bug report about Figma's prototype transitions.
>
> Someone on the team replied. We kept talking.
>
> This week I started at Figma, on the prototyping team. Now I get to fix the thing I complained about. 🎉
>
> Huge thanks to the team at my old fintech startup. Four years designing that app taught me most of what I know.

The emoji and the thank-you stay because they're normal for the occasion. The hook moved to the story, which is the part nobody else's announcement has.
